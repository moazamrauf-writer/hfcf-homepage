Islamic Giving · Ramadan

Your Ramadan Donation Gives an Orphaned Girl a Family and Multiplies Your Blessings

Ramadan is one of the most sacred times of the year for Muslims. It is the ninth month of the Islamic calendar, the month of fasting, prayer, and the Quran.

Ramadan opens the door to spiritual growth and draws families and communities closer. It is also the month of mercy and generosity. The Prophet (peace be upon him) was the most generous of people, and he was most generous of all in Ramadan (Sahih al-Bukhari 6). Following his Sunnah, believers give more and worship more in this blessed month.

This Ramadan, plan your donation so it gives orphaned girls in Pakistan what every child deserves: a safe home, Sehri and Iftar around a table full of sisters, school and care.

Section 2 · Significance

Why Is Ramadan Significant?

The Quran, the final word of Allah (SWT), was revealed in Ramadan (Surah Al-Baqarah, 2:185). The last Ashara (ten nights) is especially sacred, because it holds Laylatul Qadr, the Night of Decree, when worship is better than a thousand months.

Ramadan has also shaped Islamic history. The Battle of Badr was fought on the 17th of Ramadan, and the Conquest of Makkah took place in Ramadan, around the 20th.

In Ramadan, Allah (SWT) made fasting obligatory on the believers (Surah Al-Baqarah, 2:183). Beyond the obligatory prayers and fasting, Muslims increase their voluntary worship, and many give their Zakat, Sadaqah, and Fitrana in this month.

Section 3 · Mercy

In the Quran and Sunnah, Ramadan is a month of mercy, reward, forgiveness and safety from the Fire.

Allah’s Messenger (peace be upon him) said, “When the month of Ramadan starts, the gates of heaven are opened, the gates of Hell are closed, and the devils are chained.” Sahih al-Bukhari 1899
Section 4 · Why charity matters

Why Ramadan Charity Matters

Ramadan is the best month for charity, especially its last ten nights, when Laylatul Qadr falls. Every good deed done on that night is better than the same deed done for a thousand months. That is one of the many reasons to plan your Ramadan charity early, so that no night passes without it.

This Ramadan, give to orphaned and underprivileged girls in Pakistan. Your gift gives them a safe home, three healthy meals a day, school and healthcare, and pleases Allah (SWT) through care for the orphan, a deed He loves.

Section 8 · FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

Why Do Muslim Charities Do More Fundraising in Ramadan?+

Many Muslims choose to give their Zakat and Sadaqah in Ramadan, a month full of barakah, when every good deed is multiplied. That is why charities, including ours, focus their appeals on this blessed month.

What Is the Nisab for Zakat in Ramadan 2027?+

We’ll update this page as soon as the Nisab for 2027 is announced in Pakistan. Meanwhile, you can work it out yourself: most scholars in Pakistan take the Nisab as the market value of 612.36 grams (52.5 tola) of silver. Or use our free Zakat calculator.

Why Are the Last Ten Nights of Ramadan So Important?+

Laylatul Qadr falls in the last ten nights, and worship and charity on that night are better than a thousand months. Giving on every one of the ten nights is the surest way to give on Laylatul Qadr. Pledge your 10 nights for the girls at HFCF.

What Does the Sunnah Teach About Ramadan?+

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said, “Whoever fasts Ramadan out of faith and in hope of reward, his previous sins will be forgiven” (Sahih al-Bukhari 38). He was also more generous in Ramadan than at any other time (Sahih al-Bukhari 6).

How Long Is Ramadan?+

Ramadan lasts 29 or 30 days. It begins and ends with the sighting of the new moon.

What Is Zakat?+

Zakat is an obligatory charity on every adult Muslim whose wealth reaches the Nisab and stays above it for one lunar year. It is 2.5% of their net zakatable wealth, paid every year.

What Is Fidya?+

Fidya is paid by someone who cannot fast and will not be able to make up the fasts later, such as an elderly person or someone with a long-term illness. For each missed fast, they feed one person in need, or give the equivalent in money. Someone who misses fasts for a temporary reason, such as travel or a short illness, makes them up later and does not pay Fidya. At HFCF, Fidya is used only to feed the girls. To confirm: HFCF accepts Fidya

Who Must Pay Zakat al-Fitr (Fitrana)?+

In the Hanafi school, every Muslim who owns wealth equal to the Nisab, beyond their basic needs, on the morning of Eid must pay Fitrana for themselves and their young children. It must be paid before the Eid prayer. Your Fitrana to HFCF gives the girls food and Eid clothes.

What Is the Difference Between Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha?+

Eid al-Fitr marks the end of Ramadan, on 1 Shawwal. Eid al-Adha falls on 10 Dhul Hijjah, during the Hajj. It honours the willingness of Prophet Ibrahim (AS) to sacrifice his son Prophet Ismail (AS) for Allah (SWT).
